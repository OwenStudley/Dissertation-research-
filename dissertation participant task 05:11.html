Study Title: Individual Differences in Gaming Experience and Computer-Based Task Performance
Researcher: Owen Studley | University: Plymouth Marjon University
Supervisor: Damien Hackney

What is the purpose of the research?

You are being invited to participate in a research study investigating the relationship between individual differences in gaming experience and performance on an online reaction-time task. The study aims to explore how people perform during a task involving visual stimuli and rapid responses.

Some details about the research aims and hypotheses will not be disclosed before participation, as this could influence how participants approach the task and affect the findings. A full explanation will be provided during the debriefing at the end of the study.

What will participation involve?

  • Complete a short questionnaire about your experiences gaming.
  • Complete an online reaction-time task.
  • Respond to visual stimuli as quickly and accurately as possible.
  • Receive performance-related information during the task.

The study should take approximately 5 minutes to complete.

Risks and Withdrawal

There are no significant risks associated with participation. However, some aspects of the task may be challenging or mildly frustrating. You may also receive performance-related feedback that does not provide a complete representation of your actual performance.

Participation is entirely voluntary. You can stop participating at any time without providing a reason and without disadvantage. You may withdraw at any point before submitting your responses.

Data Protection & Confidentiality

Your responses will be used for academic research purposes. Data will be anonymised and stored securely in accordance with university procedures and relevant data protection requirements. Your data will not be used to identify you in the final research report.

At the end of the task, you will receive a debriefing explaining the full purpose of the research, including details that were withheld before participation.

Task Instructions

  1. Position yourself comfortably with your dominant index finger over the primary mouse button.
  2. Focus on the central box. A fixation cross (+) signals the start of each trial.
  3. When the box turns GREEN and displays "CLICK NOW!", click it as quickly as possible.
  4. Do not click early: Clicks during the fixation cross (+) or blank screen reset the trial.
  5. You will complete 3 practice trials first before starting the recorded main experiment.
